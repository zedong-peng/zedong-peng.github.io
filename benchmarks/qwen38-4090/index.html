Qwen3.8 27B on an RTX 4090. Artificial Analysis times how long a computer takes to serve a recorded coding agent. A 2022 gaming card finishes in nine minutes, behind only the RTX 5090.

Minutes to finish the replay. Highlighted: our RTX 4090, median of three runs; the other systems are Artificial Analysis's, October 2026.

Memory decides whether it runs, bandwidth how fast

Each step of writing reads all 16 GB of weights, so memory bandwidth sorts the table into tiers: the RTX 5090 at 1,792 GB/s, the RTX 4090 at 1,008, and the rest at 256 to 307, with room for far larger models but slow to read them.

Twenty-four gigabytes is just enough. With llama.cpp’s weights loaded, the 65,536-token cache still fits. vLLM’s 4-bit checkpoint does not: it keeps the embedding and output layers at 16 bits, loads 17.6 GiB, and leaves 2.35 GiB for a cache that needs 2.91.

An agent reads far more than it writes

Over 168 turns the agent sends 2.58 million prompt tokens and gets back 30,883. Most of each prompt was already sent the turn before and sits in the cache; the 178,000 new tokens still take nearly four minutes to read. Writing takes the rest.

Speculative decoding shortens only the writing. DFlash drafts seven tokens at a time where MTP drafts two, and writes 113 tokens a second instead of 82. It saves nearly two minutes of writing and none of the reading.

Method

Workload: AgentPerf-Local's default replay, 8 recorded coding-agent tasks and 168 turns sent one at a time with a 65,536-token context; every turn writes its recorded number of tokens, and tool time is not counted. Our runs use their tool and their recipes for the RTX 5090 (llama.cpp b11026, Q4_K_M, MTP 2) and the RTX Spark (b11236, UD-Q4_K_M, DFlash 7) on one RTX 4090. Commands and raw data.
